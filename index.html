01 / Overview

About the Field Study

An academic research initiative exploring cybersecurity readiness in educational institutions.

Educational institutions use computers, internet services, cloud platforms, student portals, digital classrooms, online examinations, and other digital systems. These systems contain important information about students, teachers, staff, and the institution.

The purpose of this field study is to understand how educational institutions protect their digital systems and data from cybersecurity threats.

Digital Security

Protecting computers, networks, applications, and digital resources.

Data Protection

Protecting student, staff, academic, and administrative information.

Cyber Awareness

Educating students and staff about safe digital practices.

07 / Risks

Common Cybersecurity Threats

Frequent threats observed in educational institutions and how to prevent them.

Phishing

Fraudulent emails or messages tricking users into revealing sensitive information.

Verify sender and avoid clicking unknown links.

Malware

Malicious software designed to damage or gain unauthorized access to systems.

Use updated antivirus and avoid unknown downloads.

Ransomware

Encrypts files and demands payment for decryption.

Maintain regular offline backups.

Weak Passwords

Simple or reused passwords make accounts vulnerable to attacks.

Use strong, unique passwords with MFA.

Social Engineering

Manipulating people into revealing confidential information.

Train users to verify identity requests.

Data Theft

Unauthorized access and theft of sensitive institutional data.

Encrypt data and restrict access rights.

Unauthorized Access

Intruders gaining access to systems without permission.

Implement strong access controls.

Unsafe Wi-Fi

Public or unsecured networks expose data to interception.

Avoid sensitive transactions on public Wi-Fi.

09 / For Students

Cybersecurity Tips for Students

Simple but powerful habits every student should adopt to stay safe online.

🔐

Use Strong Passwords

Combine letters, numbers, and symbols for better security.

📧

Beware of Phishing Emails

Verify sender details before clicking any link or attachment.

🔗

Don't Open Suspicious Links

Avoid unknown URLs, especially from strangers or pop-ups.

📱

Keep Devices Updated

Install updates to patch security vulnerabilities.

☁️

Protect Your Cloud Accounts

Enable MFA and use unique passwords for cloud services.

📶

Avoid Unsafe Public Wi-Fi

Use VPNs when connecting to public networks.

💾

Backup Important Files

Keep copies of assignments and projects in secure storage.

👤

Don't Share Passwords

Keep credentials private, even from close friends.

10 / Summary

Conclusion

Cybersecurity has become an important part of educational institutions because a large amount of academic, personal, and administrative information is stored digitally. The field study helps identify existing cybersecurity practices, awareness levels, and possible security gaps.

Educational institutions should regularly update their security policies, protect their networks and devices, conduct cybersecurity awareness programs, maintain backups, and establish proper incident-response procedures.

Cybersecurity is not only the responsibility of the IT department. Students, teachers, and staff also play an important role in maintaining a safe digital environment.

Cybersecurity is everyone's responsibility.
